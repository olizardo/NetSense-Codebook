1 Presentations, Posters & Technical Reports

This page catalogs conference posters, invited lecture slide decks, grant proposals, and empirical technical reports produced during the NetSense study. These documents detail the study design, theoretical motivations, data pipeline architecture, and initial findings on social network evolution.

1.1 Conference Posters

High-resolution conference presentation posters detailing network evolution and formation dynamics.

NoteThe Evolution of Student Networks (SoCS 2013)

David Hachen
Presented at the International Conference on Social Computing and its Applications (SoCS 2013).
Overview of the NetSense multi-modal sensing infrastructure, tracking face-to-face interactions via Bluetooth proximity and digital communication channels across the college transition.

Download Poster PDF (1.3 MB)

NoteNetwork Formation and Trajectories in a College Cohort

Lei Wang, David Hachen, & Omar Lizardo
Presented at NetSci: International School and Conference on Network Science.
Empirical analysis of tie formation mechanisms, proximity pings, and demographic homophily governing dyadic persistence over academic terms.

Download Poster PDF (1.9 MB)

1.3 Technical Reports & Working Memos

Methodological white papers and grant deliverables documenting data preparation rules, tie definitions, and structural distributions.

NoteNetwork Evolution Report: The First 45 Weeks (August 2011 – May 2012)

NetSense Research Team
A comprehensive technical report documenting the longitudinal evolution of the student network over 45 continuous academic weeks, integrating call records, text messages, Bluetooth proximity events, and survey rounds.

Download Full Report (.docx, 12 MB)

NoteDistinguishing Pre-Existing vs. College-Formed Edges in Communication Streams

David Hachen & Research Team
Report analyzing edge types in the NetSense telemetry streams to cleanly separate relationships formed prior to entering college from new friendships initiated on campus.

Download Report (.docx, 1.4 MB)

NoteNetwork Degree Distributions & Demographic Correlates

Omar Lizardo
Detailed technical monograph examining total, pre-existing, and new-tie degree distributions across student demographic attributes, Big Five personality traits, drinking behavior, and social media activity.

Download Report PDF (280 KB)

NoteTechnical Memo: Definition and Censoring of “Active Ties”

Margaret Pistorius & NetSense Team
Methodological documentation on establishing active tie thresholds, handling missing weeks, and calculating dyadic activity indicators from weekly communication matrices.

Download Memo PDF (13 KB)

NoteNSF IBSS Full Research Proposal

David Hachen, Omar Lizardo, et al.
Full NSF Interdisciplinary Behavioral and Social Science (IBSS) grant proposal outlining the theoretical mechanisms of network co-evolution, homophily, and digital phenotyping.
